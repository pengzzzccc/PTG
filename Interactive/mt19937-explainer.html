Core invariant · report §2.3

① Pseudo-random = deterministic recurrence: the seed is the initial state

The paper stresses one thing throughout: a generator is not a source of true randomness but a fixed orbit, and the seed only decides where you board. Below is splitmix64 — the generator this project actually uses — from the same deterministic-recurrence family as MT. Verify the invariant with your own hands.

Table columns: the stepped 64-bit state (each step adds the golden-ratio constant γ = 264/φ), the mixed output, and the output mapped to [0,1). The state itself is just "seed + k·γ" — output is a pure function of state. In this project it does exactly one job: shuffling the 256-entry Perlin permutation table (about 255 draws).

Paper §1.2 · Definition 1.1

② k-distribution: turning "looks random" into a checkable claim

Definition: take the top v bits of each of k consecutive values and tile the whole period — all 2kv bit patterns must occur equally often (all-zero excepted). Geometric picture: cut the unit cube into equal small cells; every cell must receive the same number of points. Below is the k = 2 two-dimensional slice — left: scatter of consecutive pairs; right: cell-occupancy heatmap. Switch generators and the difference shows instantly.

Scatter of consecutive pairs (xᵢ, xᵢ₊₁)

2^v × 2^v cell counts (a 2-D slice of k-distribution)

Every consecutive pair of the bad LCG falls exactly on the line y = 13x + 1 (mod 2¹⁶) — about 13 parallel lines, so many cells stay empty forever. MT19937 and splitmix64 hover around the expected count. Note: classic large-modulus LCGs (RANDU, for example) can fake uniformity in 2-D; their flaws only surface in 3-D (15 planes) or lagged projections — exactly why the paper prefers k-distribution, a criterion computable for any k and v.

Paper §2.1 · eq. (2.1)

③ The twist recurrence: how a new word is twisted out of old ones

The recurrence: x_{k+624} := x_{k+397} ⊕ (top 31 bits of x_k | lowest bit of x_{k+1}) × A, parameters (w, n, m, r) = (32, 624, 397, 31). The matrix A is shaped so that multiplying by it reduces to shift right by one, then XOR the constant 0x9908B0DF if the lowest bit is 1 — that is where the name Twister comes from. ④ Tempering: curing the low bits; invertible = not secure

The raw recurrence output has poor k-distribution in its low bits, so every output word passes four shift-and-mask XORs. Inversion demo: why the paper says MT is not cryptographically secure (§1.6)

Output z = x·T, and T is invertible. Given z, four inverse steps recover the raw recurrence word x; given 624 consecutive outputs you can solve for the entire internal state. The remedy the paper gives: pass output through a secure hash first whenever cryptography matters.

Paper §3.1

⑤ Why it must be a Mersenne prime: the arithmetic of 19937

state = 624 words × 32 bits = 19968 bits
the incomplete array drops r = 31 bits ⇒ 19937 bits = Mersenne-prime exponent p
⇒ period = 219937 − 1 is prime ⇒ the period proof needs no factorization
⇒ the title: ⌊19937 / 32⌋ = 623 dimensions of 32-bit-accuracy equidistribution

To prove the period reaches 2^p−1 you must verify that the order of t in (F₂[t]/φ)× is exactly 2^p−1 — one check per prime factor q of 2^p−1. If 2^p−1 is composite (p = 11, say) it must be fully factored first; if it is prime, a single check suffices — primality testing is far easier than factoring, and that is the entire motivation for choosing a Mersenne prime.

Bonus: initialization is care-free

Maximal period ⇒ primitive characteristic polynomial ⇒ all non-zero states lie on one single orbit. The differently colored seeds are merely different starting points on the same loop; the scenery along the way — the statistics of the whole period — is identical. "The choice of an initial seed does not affect the randomness", in deliberate contrast to GFSR days, when initialization was a delicate affair.
Paper §1.6

⑥ The limits the paper admits itself

The gist of §1.6: MT as it stands is not cryptographically secure — output passes through an invertible linear map only, and the inversion demo in §④ is the evidence; wrap it in a secure hash first if cryptography matters. It is designed for uniform reals in [0,1], with emphasis on the most significant bits (the generators that failed the Ising-model test had exactly high-bit defects); concatenate two words for 64-bit integers. One sentence for this project: determinism ≠ unpredictability, and the course demo only needs the former.

Defence self-test · notes §8

⑧ Seven self-test questions (think first, then expand)

1. What do the two words of the name mean?

Mersenne: the period is 2^p−1 with p a Mersenne-prime exponent; Twister: the matrix A "twists" the concatenated high-and-low bits in the recurrence (shift right, then a conditional XOR constant).

2. Where does 623 come from?

State bits nw−r = 624×32−31 = 19937, and ⌊19937/32⌋ = 623 — at 32-bit accuracy at most 623 dimensions of equidistribution; 31 bits are missing for a 624th.

3. The geometric and cryptographic readings of k-distribution?

Geometric: equal point counts in every cell of the k-dimensional unit cube. Cryptographic: knowing the top v bits of the first k−1 words tells you nothing about the top v bits of the next word.

4. Why is the number of polynomial terms a criterion?

Trinomial-family generators failed in a real simulation (the Ising model, Ferrenberg et al. 1992); the characteristic polynomial of MT has roughly 100 terms and no engineered relation to a trinomial.

5. Why is MT inherently insecure?

Output = state × invertible matrix T; T⁻¹ turns output back into the linear recurrence, and 624 consecutive outputs solve the whole state (see §④ on this page).

6. Why does the project not use MT19937?

The whole pipeline consumes about 255 draws (one table shuffle); the one-word state and zero warm-up of splitmix64 suffice. What is inherited is the invariant "seed → deterministic recurrence", not the specific algorithm.

7. Why does initialization not matter?

A primitive characteristic polynomial gives a single orbit: all non-zero states lie on it, so the seed only picks the starting point and never changes the statistics of the full period.
